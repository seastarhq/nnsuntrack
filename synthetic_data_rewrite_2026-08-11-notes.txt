Synthetic Image Generation - Realism Rewrite
2026-08-11
=======================================================================

Rewrite of synthetic_data_script.py for physical realism, plus the
Makefile changes that go with it.


-----------------------------------------------------------
Core change
-----------------------------------------------------------

Scenes are now built in linear radiance (float32) and pushed through the
imaging chain in the order light actually meets it:

    scene radiance -> optics (bloom, flare, vignetting)
                   -> exposure/gain
                   -> sensor (PRNU, hot pixels/rows, shot + read noise)
                   -> 8-bit quantization

Previously everything was drawn directly in 8-bit. That is what makes
the bright cases behave: the sun is ~1e4 times brighter than the sky, so
it saturates and its point-spread wings bloom into a blob far larger
than the disk itself. A flat-topped disk drawn at brightness 200 cannot
reproduce that.


-----------------------------------------------------------
Sun size - now geometric, not arbitrary
-----------------------------------------------------------

Driven by --sun_angular_diameter (0.53 deg default) through a camera
model rather than a free pixel range:

    narrow  (30 deg, rectilinear)   13.8 px across
    fisheye (140 deg, equidistant)   3.0 px across

Both verified against hand arithmetic. The old 20-100 px *radius* range
was 3-30x oversized.

Still fully overridable for a hardware change:
    --fov_deg  --projection  --image_width/height
    --sun_radius_px      force a fixed pixel radius
    --sun_size_scale     multiplier on angular diameter

Off-axis anisotropy falls out of the projection correctly, and in
opposite directions for the two lenses (semi-axis ratio at frame edge):

    rectilinear  0.957   sun stretched radially
    equidistant  1.401   sun stretched tangentially


-----------------------------------------------------------
Bright cloud edges
-----------------------------------------------------------

Forward-scattered sunlight through a thin cloud edge is modelled as
peaking at moderate optical depth (thick cloud blocks it, clear sky has
nothing to scatter) and falling off with distance from the sun. The edge
genuinely outshines the solar disk, so "track the brightest blob" is the
wrong policy - while the label keeps pointing at the sun.

Occlusion is computed from mean transmission over the solar disk, giving
a continuous label rather than a binary flag.

Measured effect - brightest-blob tracker centroid error:

    sun_only            median   0.7 px
    bright_cloud_edge   median  56.9 px,  50% of frames off by >20 px
    occluded_sun        median   0.6 px

Relevant knobs:
    --forward_scatter_gain   brightness of the edge glow
    --forward_scatter_scale  falloff distance in px
    --cloud_optical_depth  --cloud_edge_softness
    --cloud_thickness_scale  --cloud_shade_range


-----------------------------------------------------------
Realism items added
-----------------------------------------------------------

  - Multi-scale PSF bloom (sharp core + broad veiling-glare wings).
    One PSF for everything: bloom is a property of the lens, so the same
    convolution applies to the sun and to glints. The sun blooms more
    only because it is brighter.
  - Lens flare ghosts along the sun-to-optical-centre axis.
  - Hot pixels and hot rows, fixed-pattern: generated once per dataset
    from --seed so they sit at the same pixels in every frame.
    Re-rolling them per image would make them ordinary noise.
  - PRNU (per-pixel gain variation), dark offset.
  - Poisson shot noise scaling with signal, plus Gaussian read noise.
    Replaces the old uniform additive Gaussian.
  - cos^4 vignetting (gentler falloff for the fisheye).
  - Sky brightness gradient; cloud fBm noise with backlit shading, so
    thin parts glow and thick cores go dark.
  - Fixed and auto-exposure regimes, mixed by default (--exposure).

Starburst / diffraction spikes are OFF by default (--starburst to
enable). The Arducam has a fixed aperture and no diaphragm blades, so it
cannot produce them - that was a DSLR artifact and was removed.


-----------------------------------------------------------
New label columns in metadata.csv
-----------------------------------------------------------

    sun_present     1.0 whenever a sun is in the scene, even fully
                    hidden - separates "no sun here" from "sun present
                    but occluded"
    occlusion       0.0 clear .. 1.0 fully hidden, NaN when no sun
    exposure_mode   'auto' or 'fixed'
    exposure_gain   gain applied to that frame

confidence keeps its previous meaning (1.0 clear, mean transmission when
occluded, 0.0 when no sun). Centroids stay labelled through occlusion,
including when the sun is fully hidden, so tracking continues.

Columns are additive, so training_script.py loads the data unchanged.
A generation_params.json is also written to each dataset directory,
recording camera geometry - otherwise unrecoverable from the PNGs.


-----------------------------------------------------------
Three defects found by inspecting the output
-----------------------------------------------------------

Each would have quietly degraded the dataset:

  1. Auto-exposure metered on the 99th percentile, so a single
     saturating glint drove gain to its floor and crushed the whole sky
     to black. Now center-weighted average metering.
  2. Reflections at 20-100 px were enormous and hard-edged - cartoon
     polygons. Now 5-45 px and sharing the lens PSF.
  3. Clouds were flat binary blobs. Now soft-edged with backlit shading.

Also: fixed-exposure gain was sampled over a 10x range, producing frames
57% saturated to white that were still labelled confidence=1.0 with a
centroid - actively harmful training data. Fixed exposure now samples a
narrow range (a real fixed exposure is set for the sky); auto-exposure
keeps the wide range as clip limits. No frame in 120 now exceeds 20%
saturation.


-----------------------------------------------------------
Makefile
-----------------------------------------------------------

CAMERA ?= narrow now drives --camera and moves DATA_DIR and MODEL to
*_$(CAMERA), so the two cameras never overwrite each other:

    make train CAMERA=fisheye

One dataset and one model per camera: the narrow lens does 0.1-degree
tracking, the fisheye does sky orientation.


-----------------------------------------------------------
Cost
-----------------------------------------------------------

~62 ms/image, so a 70000-image dataset takes ~72 minutes - up from ~2
minutes, and now comparable to training time. That is the price of the
radiance pipeline. The per-image work is embarrassingly parallel; a
multiprocessing.Pool would cut it to a few minutes.


-----------------------------------------------------------
Verified
-----------------------------------------------------------

  - Both camera presets generate; disk sizes match hand calculation.
  - Off-axis anisotropy correct and opposite for the two projections.
  - Label distributions sane across all seven scene types.
  - Bright-cloud effect defeats brightest-blob tracking as intended.
  - Visual inspection of contact sheets at each iteration.
  - Full `make smoke` end-to-end on GPU: data -> train -> tflite ->
    inference, output confidence=0.517 centroid=(360.41, 276.91).


-----------------------------------------------------------
Outstanding
-----------------------------------------------------------

1. The occlusion / sun_present labels are inert. training_script.py
   outputs 3 values and its y is 4-wide (4th is a loss mask). Consuming
   the new signal means a 5-wide label, a new head, a loss change, and
   matching updates in metrics/visualize/deployment. That last one
   changes run_inference's return signature, which the ROS2 node
   imports.

2. Clouds and glints are drawn in image space without a projection warp,
   so the fisheye's radial stretching is not imprinted on them.
   Defensible since both are procedurally random, but it is a real
   limitation.
